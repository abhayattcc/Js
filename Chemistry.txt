
CHEMISTRY

1.  Which branch of science is concerned with the composition, properties, characteristics, and reactions of substances along with energy changes?
    a) Biology
    b) Physics
    c) Chemistry
    d) Geology
    Answer: c) Chemistry

2.  Inorganic Chemistry is associated with the properties of compounds of the chemical elements contained in:
    a) Living organisms
    b) Minerals
    c) Carbon
    d) Water
    Answer: b) Minerals

3.  Organic Chemistry is concerned with the compounds of:
    a) Nitrogen and Oxygen
    b) Carbon and Hydrogen or their derivatives
    c) Metals and Metalloids
    d) Noble Gases
    Answer: b) Carbon and Hydrogen or their derivatives

STATES OF MATTER

4.  Anything that occupies space and has mass or weight is called:
    a) Energy
    b) Matter
    c) Light
    d) Sound
    Answer: b) Matter

5.  Matter undergoes changes which may be:
    a) Only physical changes
    b) Only chemical changes
    c) Either chemical changes or physical changes
    d) Biological changes
    Answer: c) Either chemical changes or physical changes

6.  All matters exist in one of the three physical forms or states. These are:
    a) Solid, liquid, and plasma
    b) Solid, liquid, and gas
    c) Liquid, gas, and Bose-Einstein condensate
    d) Solid, plasma, and gas
    Answer: b) Solid, liquid, and gas

7.  The three states of matter are:
    a) Non-interconvertible
    b) Interconvertible
    c) Only convert from solid to liquid
    d) Only convert from liquid to gas
    Answer: b) Interconvertible

KINETIC THEORY OF MATTER

8.  All forms of matter are composed of small:
    a) Energy packets
    b) Waves
    c) Particles (molecules, atoms, ions)
    d) Fields
    Answer: c) Particles (molecules, atoms, ions)

9.  The particles of matter are in continuous, never-ending motion, also known as:
    a) Random walk
    b) Orbital motion
    c) Brownian motion
    d) Vibrational motion
    Answer: c) Brownian motion

10. According to the kinetic theory of matter, the higher the temperature, the faster is the motion of particles, as thermal energy is converted into:
    a) Potential energy
    b) Electrical energy
    c) Kinetic energy
    d) Chemical energy
    Answer: c) Kinetic energy

11. The particles of matter attract one another with a force called:
    a) Gravitational force
    b) Electrostatic force
    c) Intermolecular force
    d) Nuclear force
    Answer: c) Intermolecular force

12. Intermolecular force in matter:
    a) Increases with increasing distance between particles
    b) Decreases with increasing distance between particles
    c) Remains constant regardless of distance
    d) Is only present in solids
    Answer: b) Decreases with increasing distance between particles

CHARACTERISTICS OF MATTER

13. In a solid, the particles are:
    a) Far apart and move freely
    b) Packed closely together, so inter-particle attractive force is very high
    c) Randomly distributed with weak forces
    d) Constantly changing their positions
    Answer: b) Packed closely together, so inter-particle attractive force is very high

14. In liquids, the inter-particle force is:
    a) Very high, preventing flow
    b) Negligible, allowing free wandering
    c) Weak, allowing them to flow and occupy the shape of the container
    d) Non-existent
    Answer: c) Weak, allowing them to flow and occupy the shape of the container

15. In gases, the inter-particle force is:
    a) Very high
    b) Strong
    c) Negligible
    d) Moderate
    Answer: c) Negligible

16. The free random motion of particles in gases is called:
    a) Orbital motion
    b) Vibrational motion
    c) Brownian motion
    d) Translational motion
    Answer: c) Brownian motion

CHANGES IN STATES OF MATTER

17. Heating a solid increases the kinetic energy and consequently motion of the particles, leading to:
    a) Increased rigidity
    b) Loss of the rigid structure
    c) Stronger intermolecular forces
    d) Decreased volume
    Answer: b) Loss of the rigid structure

18. When heat is removed from a gas (vapour), the kinetic energy of the particles is reduced, causing them to:
    a) Speed up and move further apart
    b) Become slower and come nearer
    c) Remain unaffected
    d) Convert directly into a solid
    Answer: b) Become slower and come nearer

19. The process where a gas becomes a liquid due to reduced kinetic energy and increased inter-particle attractive forces is called:
    a) Evaporation
    b) Sublimation
    c) Condensation
    d) Melting
    Answer: c) Condensation

20. The process where a solid changes directly into a gas on heating without undergoing the liquid state, or vice versa, is called:
    a) Evaporation
    b) Melting
    c) Freezing
    d) Sublimation
    Answer: d) Sublimation

21. Sublimation occurs when the attractive forces between the particles are:
    a) Very high
    b) Moderate
    c) Low
    d) Non-existent
    Answer: c) Low

CLASSIFICATION OF MATTER

ELEMENTS

22. A pure substance that is composed of only one kind of atoms is called an:
    a) Compound
    b) Mixture
    c) Element
    d) Solution
    Answer: c) Element

23. An element cannot be composed from or decomposed into simpler substances by:
    a) Chemical means only
    b) Physical means only
    c) A physical or chemical change
    d) Nuclear reactions only
    Answer: c) A physical or chemical change

24. Which of the following is an example of an element?
    a) Water (H2O)
    b) Sodium Chloride (NaCl)
    c) Aluminium (Al)
    d) Silicon Dioxide (SiO2)
    Answer: c) Aluminium (Al)

25. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of metals?
    a) Metallic Lustre
    b) Good thermal and electrical conductivity
    c) Ductility
    d) Absence of malleability
    Answer: d) Absence of malleability

26. The property of metals that allows them to be drawn into wires is called:
    a) Malleability
    b) Brittleness
    c) Ductility
    d) Conductivity
    Answer: c) Ductility

27. The property of metals that allows them to be beaten into sheets is called:
    a) Ductility
    b) Lustre
    c) Malleability
    d) Conductivity
    Answer: c) Malleability

28. Which metal is known for being the most ductile and malleable, making it suitable for ornaments?
    a) Copper

29. Non-metals are characterized by:
    a) Metallic lustre

30. Elements that exhibit properties midway between those of metals and non-metals are called:
    a) Non-conductors

COMPOUNDS

38. A pure substance that is composed of two or more elements chemically combined in definite and constant proportions is called a:
    a) Mixture
    b) Element
    c) Compound
    d) Solution
    Answer: c) Compound

39. Which of the following is an example of a compound?
    a) Iron (Fe)
    b) Hydrogen (H)
    c) Water (H2O)
    d) Aluminium (Al)
    Answer: c) Water (H2O)

40. The separation of a compound into its constituent elements by chemical means is called:
    a) Synthesis

41. The formation of a compound by the union of elements is called:
    a) Analysis

42. The most commonly found compound on the Earth's crust in the lithosphere is:
    a) Water (H2O)

43. The most commonly found element on Earth is:
    a) Silicon

45. What is the atomicity of NaCl?

MIXTURES

47. Matter composed of two or more substances (elements, compounds or both) in variable proportions in which each of its components still exhibits its own characteristic properties is called a:
    a) Compound
    b) Element
    c) Mixture
    d) Pure substance
    Answer: c) Mixture

48. Mixtures can be:
    a) Only homogeneous
    b) Only heterogeneous
    c) Heterogeneous or homogeneous
    d) Only solutions
    Answer: c) Heterogeneous or homogeneous

49. A mixture with the same composition throughout the sample is called a:
    a) Heterogeneous mixture
    b) Homogeneous mixture
    c) Suspension
    d) Colloid
    Answer: b) Homogeneous mixture

50. An example of a homogeneous mixture is:
    a) Mixture of iron and sulfur
    b) Salt solution
    c) Sand and water
    d) Oil and water
    Answer: b) Salt solution

51. A mixture consisting of two or more parts which have different compositions is called a:
    a) Homogeneous mixture
    b) True solution
    c) Heterogeneous mixture
    d) Colloid
    Answer: c) Heterogeneous mixture

52. An example of a heterogeneous mixture is:
    a) Air
    b) Sugar in water
    c) Mixture of iron and sulfur
    d) Brass
    Answer: c) Mixture of iron and sulfur

TYPES OF COLLOIDS

53. What is the dispersion medium for a solid sol?
    a) Liquid
    b) Gas
    c) Solid
    d) Air
    Answer: c) Solid

54. Minerals and rocks, and gems are examples of which type of colloid?
    a) Sol
    b) Emulsion
    c) Solid sol
    d) Gel
    Answer: c) Solid sol

55. Gold sol and sulfur sol are examples of colloids where the dispersed phase is solid and the dispersion medium is:
    a) Gas
    b) Solid
    c) Liquid
    d) Air
    Answer: c) Liquid

56. Smoke is an example of a colloid where the dispersed phase is solid and the dispersion medium is:
    a) Liquid
    b) Solid
    c) Gas
    d) Water
    Answer: c) Gas

57. Milk is an example of an emulsion, where the dispersed phase and dispersion medium are both:
    a) Solid
    b) Gas
    c) Liquid
    d) Solid and liquid respectively
    Answer: c) Liquid

58. Cheese and jellies are examples of gels, where the dispersed phase is liquid and the dispersion medium is:
    a) Gas
    b) Liquid
    c) Solid
    d) Air
    Answer: c) Solid

59. Clouds are examples of liquid aerosol, where the dispersed phase is liquid and the dispersion medium is:
    a) Solid
    b) Liquid
    c) Gas
    d) Water
    Answer: c) Gas

60. Foam and froth in air are examples of colloids where the dispersed phase is gas and the dispersion medium is:
    a) Solid
    b) Liquid
    c) Gas
    d) Water
    Answer: b) Liquid

61. Pumice stone is an example of a solid foam, where the dispersed phase is gas and the dispersion medium is:
    a) Liquid
    b) Gas
    c) Solid
    d) Air
    Answer: c) Solid

SOLUTIONS, SOLUTE AND SOLVENT

66. A homogenous mixture of two or more substances is called a:
    a) Compound
    b) Element
    c) Solution
    d) Suspension
    Answer: c) Solution

67. In a solution, the substance which is dissolved is called the:
    a) Solvent
    b) Solute
    c) Medium
    d) Precipitate
    Answer: b) Solute

68. In a solution, the liquid in which the solute is dissolved is known as the:
    a) Solute
    b) Solution
    c) Solvent
    d) Mixture
    Answer: c) Solvent

69. True solutions are always:
    a) Heterogeneous
    b) Homogenous
    c) Colloidal
    d) Suspensions
    Answer: b) Homogenous

70. The size of solute particles in a true solution is less than:
    a) $10^{-5}$ cm
    b) $10^{-7}$ cm
    c) $10^{-9}$ cm
    d) $10^{-3}$ cm
    Answer: b) $10^{-7}$ cm

71. In a true solution, the solute particles can:
    a) Be easily distinguished from solvent molecules using a microscope.
    b) Be distinguished from solvent molecules only by the naked eye.
    c) Not be distinguished from the solvent molecules even by using a microscope.
    d) Form a separate layer.
    Answer: c) Not be distinguished from the solvent molecules even by using a microscope.

72. A kind of solution in which the size of solute particles is bigger than that of a true solution but smaller than those of a suspension is called a:
    a) True solution
    b) Precipitate
    c) Colloid
    d) Compound
    Answer: c) Colloid

73. Which of the following is an example of a colloidal solution?
    a) Salt solution
    b) Sugar solution
    c) Milk
    d) Air
    Answer: c) Milk

74. The scattering of light by colloidal particles is called:
    a) Brownian motion
    b) Diffusion
    c) Tyndall effect
    d) Osmosis
    Answer: c) Tyndall effect

75. Strength of a solution is defined as the amount of the solute in grams found dissolved in:
    a) One milliliter of solution
    b) One kilogram of solvent
    c) One litre (or dm$^3$) of solution
    d) One gram of solvent
    Answer: c) One litre (or dm$^3$) of solution

76. Strength of a solution is expressed in:
    a) g/mol
    b) mol/L
    c) g/litre or g/dm$^3$
    d) kg/m$^3$
    Answer: c) g/litre or g/dm$^3$

77. Molarity of a solution is defined as the number of moles of the solute dissolved per:
    a) Kilogram of solvent
    b) Gram of solution
    c) Litre (or dm$^3$) of solution
    d) Milliliter of solution
    Answer: c) Litre (or dm$^3$) of solution

78. Molarity is denoted by:
    a) m
    b) M
    c) N

    Answer: b) M

79. A substance known for its specific properties such as boiling point, melting point, density, refractive index, etc., is called a:
    a) Mixture
    b) Compound

    Answer: c) Pure substance

80. To obtain salt from sea water, which of the following methods are used?
    a) Fractional distillation and chromatography
    b) Evaporation and crystallization
    c) Sublimation and filtration
    d) Centrifugation and gravity separation
    Answer: b) Evaporation and crystallization

81. Which method is used to separate different fractions of petroleum such as gasoline, diesel, kerosene oil, etc., from crude oil?
    a) Simple distillation
    b) Fractional distillation
    c) Sublimation
    d) Filtration
    Answer: b) Fractional distillation

82. To separate cream from raw milk, which process is used?
    a) Filtration
    b) Distillation
    c) Centrifugation
    d) Evaporation
    Answer: c) Centrifugation

83. Which method is used to separate substances like camphor that sublimate on heating?
    a) Distillation

    Answer: c) Sublimation

84. Filtration is used to separate:
    a) Soluble substances from a mixture
    b) Insoluble substances from a mixture

    Answer: b) Insoluble substances from a mixture

85. To separate two immiscible liquids such as oil and water, which method is employed, often using a separating funnel?
    a) Distillation
    b) Centrifugation
    c) Gravity separation

    Answer: c) Gravity separation

86. To separate potable water from marine water, the process of:
    a) Distillation

PROPERTIES OF GASES AND GAS LAWS

Kinetic-Molecular Theory of Gases

87. According to the Kinetic-Molecular theory of gases, the total volume of the molecules is negligible compared to the volume of the gas as a whole because:
    a) Molecules are very large
    b) Molecules are closely packed
    c) Gases consist of molecules widely separated in space
    d) Gases have no volume
    Answer: c) Gases consist of molecules widely separated in space

88. A gas occupies both the shape and volume of its:
    a) Molecules
    b) Pressure
    c) Container
    d) Temperature
    Answer: c) Container

Boyle’s Law

89. Boyle's Law states that the volume (V) of a given mass of a dry gas is inversely proportional to the pressure (P), if the:
    a) Volume remains constant
    b) Temperature remains constant
    c) Mass remains constant
    d) Pressure remains constant
    Answer: b) Temperature remains constant

90. Which of the following equations represents Boyle's Law?
    a) V $\propto$ T
    b) P $\propto$ V
    c) P1V1 = P2V2
    d) V/T = constant
    Answer: c) P1V1 = P2V2

Charles’s Law

91. Charles's Law states that at constant pressure, the volume (V) of a given mass of gas is directly proportional to the:
    a) Celsius temperature
    b) Absolute (Kelvin) temperature
    c) Pressure
    d) Density
    Answer: b) Absolute (Kelvin) temperature

92. Charles's Law explains that gases expand on:
    a) Cooling
    b) Heating
    c) Increasing pressure
    d) Decreasing pressure
    Answer: b) Heating

93. Hot air is less dense than cool air because:
    a) Its mass increases
    b) Its volume decreases
    c) The larger volume of gas at high temperature has less mass per unit volume
    d) It undergoes condensation
    Answer: c) The larger volume of gas at high temperature has less mass per unit volume

94. Hot air balloons rise up in cool air or humid air due to:
    a) Increased density of hot air
    b) Lower density of hot air
    c) Charles's Law not applying
    d) Condensation of water vapor
    Answer: b) Lower density of hot air

Ideal Gas

95. A gas that strictly obeys Boyle's law, Charles's law, and Avogadro's law is called an:
    a) Real gas
    b) Non-ideal gas
    c) Ideal gas
    d) Noble gas
    Answer: c) Ideal gas

96. The combined gas law states that P1V1/T1 = P2V2/T2. This leads to the ideal gas equation:
    a) P/V = nRT
    b) PV = nRT
    c) PT = nRV
    d) VT = nRP
    Answer: b) PV = nRT

97. In the ideal gas equation PV = nRT, R represents:
    a) Pressure
    b) Volume
    c) Temperature
    d) Work done per degree per mol or energy per degree per mole
    Answer: d) Work done per degree per mol or energy per degree per mole

98. The numerical value of the gas constant R is approximately:
    a) 8.314 J mol-1 K-1
    b) 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1
    c) 6.023 x 10^23
    d) 1.6 x 10^-19 C
    Answer: b) 0.0821 L atm K-1 mol-1

Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressure

99. Dalton's law of partial pressure states that the pressure of a homogenous mixture of gases like air is equal to the:
    a) Product of the partial pressures of individual gases
    b) Difference of the partial pressures of individual gases
    c) Sum of the partial pressure of individual gas present
    d) Average of the partial pressures of individual gases
    Answer: c) Sum of the partial pressure of individual gas present

Non-ideal Gas

100. A real gas, also called a non-ideal gas, does not follow all general gas equations at all:
    a) Temperatures only
    b) Pressures only
    c) Temperatures and pressure
    d) Volumes
    Answer: c) Temperatures and pressure

Diffusion and Osmosis

101. The movement of fluid particles (liquid or gas) from the region of high concentration to the region of low concentration is called:
    a) Osmosis
    b) Evaporation
    c) Diffusion
    d) Condensation
    Answer: c) Diffusion

102. Osmosis is a special type of diffusion that involves the movement of:
    a) Solute particles only
    b) Solvent particles only across a membrane
    c) Both solute and solvent particles
    d) Gas particles only
    Answer: b) Solvent particles only across a membrane

103. In osmosis, solvent particles move from a:
    a) Hypertonic solution to a hypotonic solution
    b) Isotonic solution to a hypertonic solution
    c) Hypotonic solution to a hypertonic solution
    d) Hypertonic solution to an isotonic solution
    Answer: c) Hypotonic solution to a hypertonic solution
