    a) One day

    d) One hour
    Answer: c) One year

    a) Sun
    b) Polaris

    Answer: c) Alpha Centauri

Stars

14. Stars are heavenly bodies like the Sun that are:
    a) Extremely cold and have no light of their own
    b) Extremely hot and have light of their own
    c) Made up of only dust
    d) Only visible during the day
    Answer: b) Extremely hot and have light of their own

15. Stars are made up of vast clouds of:
    a) Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide
    b) Nitrogen and Argon
    c) Hydrogen gas, helium and dust
    d) Water vapor and ice
    Answer: c) Hydrogen gas, helium and dust

16. The star which lies immediately above the Earth’s North Pole and around which all other stars seem to revolve is called:
    a) Sirius
    b) Betelgeuse
    c) Pole Star
    d) Alpha Centauri
    Answer: c) Pole Star

17. The first step in the formation of a star from gases is the:
    a) Red Giant
    b) White Dwarf
    c) Protostar
    d) Neutron Star
    Answer: c) Protostar

18. A protostar is formed by the:
    a) Explosion of a supernova
    b) Gravitational contraction of gases
    c) Expansion of a red giant
    d) Formation of a black hole
    Answer: b) Gravitational contraction of gases

19. What happens to a star's outer region when it becomes a Red Giant?
    a) It shrinks
    b) It expands
    c) It remains the same
    d) It disappears
    Answer: b) It expands

20. When the brightness of a star increases suddenly by ten to twenty magnitudes or more due to a partial or outright explosion, it is called a:
    a) Protostar
    b) Red Giant
    c) Novae/Supernovae
    d) White Dwarf
    Answer: c) Novae/Supernovae

21. When the brightness of a star increases to 20 magnitudes or more, it is called a:
    a) Novae
    b) Supernovae
    c) Protostar
    d) Red Giant
    Answer: b) Supernovae

22. A Novae/Supernovae explosion in a small star (stars lighter than 1.2 solar mass) may leave behind a very dense core that becomes a:
    a) Neutron Star
    b) Black Hole
    c) White Dwarf
    d) Pulsar
    Answer: c) White Dwarf

23. A supernovae explosion in a star bigger than the Sun but not more than twice as big, may leave behind an extremely dense, residual core known as a:
    a) White Dwarf
    b) Black Hole
    c) Neutron Star
    d) Pulsar
    Answer: c) Neutron Star

24. A spinning neutron star that emits radio waves is called a:
    a) Black Hole
    b) White Dwarf
    c) Pulsar
    d) Red Giant
    Answer: c) Pulsar

25. An object with such a strong gravitational field that even light cannot escape from its surface is called a:
    a) Neutron Star
    b) White Dwarf
    c) Pulsar
    d) Black Hole
    Answer: d) Black Hole

26. Black holes are formed from neutron stars after the:
    a) Protostar phase
    b) Red Giant phase

72. An astronomical event that occurs when an astronomical object is temporarily obscured, either by passing into the shadow of another body or by having another body pass between it and the viewer, is called an:

Solar Eclipse

73. A solar eclipse occurs when:
    a) The Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon.
    b) The Moon passes in front of the Sun as observed from Earth.
    c) The Sun passes in front of the Moon.
    d) The Moon passes behind the Earth.
    Answer: b) The Moon passes in front of the Sun as observed from Earth.

74. A total solar eclipse occurs when the Earth intersects the ______ portion of the Moon’s shadow.
    a) Penumbra
    b) Antumbra
    c) Umbra
    d) Perigee
    Answer: c) Umbra

75. When the umbra does not reach the surface of the Earth, the Sun is only partially occulted, resulting in an:
    a) Total solar eclipse
    b) Partial solar eclipse
    c) Annular eclipse
    d) Lunar eclipse

    Answer: c) Penumbra

EARTH’S MOVEMENTS

1.  Earth rotates on its own axis, which is tilted at an angle of:
    a) 0°
    b) 90°
    c) 23½° to the vertical
    d) 66½° to the vertical
    Answer: c) 23½° to the vertical

2.  Earth spins from:
    a) East to west
    b) West to east
    c) North to south
    d) South to north
    Answer: b) West to east

3.  How long does it take for the Earth to complete one rotation on its axis?
    a) 24 hours
    b) 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4.9 seconds
    c) 365 days
    d) 27 days
    Answer: b) 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4.9 seconds

4.  The linear velocity of Earth's rotation is maximum at the:
    a) Poles
    b) Tropics
    c) Equator
    d) Mid-latitudes
    Answer: c) Equator

5.  What is the approximate maximum linear velocity of Earth's rotation at the equator?
    a) 0 km/hr
    b) 100 km/hr
    c) 1690 km/hr
    d) 106,560 km/hr
    Answer: c) 1690 km/hr

6.  Which of the following is an effect of Earth’s rotation?
    a) Change of seasons
    b) Variation in the lengths of day and night
    c) Deflection of ocean currents and winds
    d) Shifting of wind belts
    Answer: c) Deflection of ocean currents and winds

7.  The difference in time between two meridians which are 15° apart is:
    a) 30 minutes
    b) 1 hour

    d) 4 hours
    Answer: b) 1 hour

    a) 24 hours

    Answer: c) 365¼ days

9.  Earth revolves on an elliptical orbit at a speed of:
    a) 1690 km/hr

    c) 300,000 km/sec
    d) 29.8 km/second
    Answer: b) 106,560 km/hr

10. An extra day is added every four years in a leap year due to:
    a) The Earth's rotation speed

11. Which of the following is an effect of Earth’s revolution?
    a) Day and night
    b) Rise and fall of tides
    c) Deflection of ocean currents

12. The variation in the lengths of day and night at different times of the year is an effect of Earth's:

    a) Sediment and clay
