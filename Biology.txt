
BIOLOGY

1.  Biology is concerned with the study of life and living organisms, including their:
    a) Structure, function, growth, evolution, distribution, and environment
    b) Only structure and function
    c) Only evolution and distribution
    d) Non-living components
    Answer: a) Structure, function, growth, evolution, distribution and environment.

2.  In general, biology recognizes the cell as the basic unit of:
    a) Heredity
    b) Evolution
    c) Life
    d) Growth
    Answer: c) life

3.  In biology, genes are considered the basic unit of:
    a) Life
    b) Evolution
    c) Function
    d) Heredity
    Answer: d) heredity

4.  What is considered the "engine that propels the creation of new species" in biology?
    a) Growth
    b) Function
    c) Evolution
    d) Distribution
    Answer: c) evolution

BIOLOGICAL CLASSIFICATION

5.  Which Greek philosopher proposed a two-kingdom system for biodiversity?
    a) Ernst Heackel
    b) Copeland
    c) R. Whittaker
    d) Aristotle
    Answer: d) Aristotle

6.  In 1866, Ernst Haeckel proposed a:
    a) Two-kingdom system
    b) Three-kingdom system
    c) Four-kingdom system
    d) Five-kingdom system
    Answer: b) three kingdom system

7.  Ernst Haeckel's three-kingdom system included:
    a) Monera, Protista, Plantae
    b) Plantae, Animalia, Protista
    c) Fungi, Plantae, Animalia
    d) Protista, Fungi, Monera
    Answer: b) Plantae, Animalia and Protista

8.  Why did Copeland propose a four-kingdom system in 1956?
    a) To add a new kingdom for fungi
    b) Because the Protista kingdom consisted of both prokaryotes and eukaryotes
    c) To simplify the classification
    d) To remove the Animalia kingdom
    Answer: b) As protista kingdom consisted of both prokaryotes and eucarytoes

9.  Copeland's four-kingdom system included:
    a) Plantae, Animalia, Protista, Fungi
    b) Monera, Protista, Plantae, Animalia
    c) Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia
    d) Monera, Protista, Fungi, Animalia
    Answer: b) Monera Protista Planatae Animalia

10. Who proposed the five-kingdom system in 1969?
    a) Aristotle
    b) Ernst Haeckel
    c) Copeland
    d) R. Whittaker
    Answer: d) R. Whittaker (1969)

11. R. Whittaker's five-kingdom system used which three criteria?
    a) Size, color, and habitat
    b) Complexity of cell structure, body structure, and mode of nutrition
    c) Locomotion, reproduction, and respiration
    d) Number of organelles, presence of cell wall, and type of DNA
    Answer: b) complexity of cell structure, body structure and mode of nutrition

12. In the five-kingdom system, which kingdom was separated out from Plantae?
    a) Protista
    b) Monera
    c) Fungi
    d) Animalia
    Answer: c) kingdom fungi from plantae.

13. The five-kingdom system includes:
    a) Monera, Protista, Plantae, Animalia, Viruses
    b) Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia, Bacteria
    c) Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia
    d) Fungi, Plantae, Animalia, Viruses, Bacteria
    Answer: c) Monera Protista Fungi Plantae Animalia

14. Who introduced the system of 'Binomial Nomenclature' and is also called the "Father of Taxonomy"?
    a) R. Whittaker
    b) Ernst Haeckel
    c) Carl Linnaeus
    d) Aristotle
    Answer: c) Carlous Linneaus

15. According to Binomial Nomenclature, the names of living organisms are written in which language?
    a) English
    b) Greek
    c) Latin
    d) German
    Answer: c) Latin language

16. In Binomial Nomenclature, a name consists of two parts:
    a) Specific epithet followed by generic name
    b) Generic name (starting with capital letter) followed by specific epithet (name of species)
    c) Only generic name
    d) Only specific epithet
    Answer: b) generic name to be written first starting with capital letter followed by specific epithet i.e. name of species.

17. In the example "Panthera leo", what does "Panthera" represent?
    a) The species name
    b) The generic name
    c) The common name
    d) The family name
    Answer: b) the generic name

18. In the example "Panthera leo", what does "leo" represent?
    a) The generic name
    b) The family name
    c) The specific epithet (name of species)
    d) The common name
    Answer: c) the species Panthera leo

CYTOLOGY

19. Who first observed a cell in 1665?
    a) Antonie van Leeuwenhoek
    b) Robert Hooke
    c) Matthias Schleiden
    d) Theodor Schwann
    Answer: b) Robert Hooke

20. The cell is considered the structural and functional unit of:
    a) Heredity
    b) Genes
    c) Life
    d) Organelles
    Answer: c) life

21. The cell is also considered a hereditary unit with genes on DNA in:
    a) Cytoplasm
    b) Cell membrane
    c) Chromosome
    d) Mitochondria
    Answer: c) chromosome

22. In most plants and animals, cells with various membrane-bound structures like mitochondria, chloroplasts, and a nucleus are known as:
    a) Prokaryotes
    b) Eukaryotes
    c) Viruses
    d) Bacteria
    Answer: b) an eukaryote.

23. Which of the following is a non-membrane bound organelle found in eukaryotic cells?
    a) Mitochondria
    b) Chloroplast
    c) Ribosomes
    d) Nucleus
    Answer: c) ribosomes and nucleolus.

24. Each cell organelle in a eukaryotic cell performs a:
    a) Random function
    b) General function
    c) Specific function
    d) Non-essential function
    Answer: c) specific function of the cell in a well organised manner.

25. In bacteria, mycoplasma, and blue-green algae cells, which of the following is lacking?
    a) Cytoplasm
    b) Cell wall
    c) Definite cell organelles and a well-defined nucleus
    d) Ribosomes
    Answer: c) definite cell organelles and the nucleoplasm and cytoplasm is not separated i.e. well defined nucleus is lacking in them.

26. Cells that lack definite cell organelles and a well-defined nucleus are known as:
    a) Eukaryotes
    b) Prokaryotes
    c) Plant cells
    d) Animal cells
    Answer: b) a prokaryote.

27. The absence of membrane-bound structures within a prokaryotic cell simply shows that they are:
    a) Highly evolved cells
    b) Primitive cells
    c) Highly specialized cells
    d) Complex cells
    Answer: b) primitive cells.

Structure and Function of Cell Organelles

28. Which organelle is found in plant cells only and is mainly made up of cellulose and hemicellulose?
    a) Nucleus
    b) Mitochondria
    c) Cell Wall
    d) Plastids
    Answer: c) Cell Wall

29. The Cell Wall provides:
    a) Flexibility and elasticity to the cell
    b) Shape, strength and rigidity to the cells
    c) Movement to the cell
    d) Digestion of waste
    Answer: b) shape, strength and rigidity to the cells.

30. In fungi, the cell wall is mainly made up of:
    a) Cellulose
    b) Hemicellulose
    c) Chitin
    d) Protein
    Answer: c) chitin (fungus)

31. In bacteria, the cell wall is mainly made up of:
    a) Cellulose
    b) Hemicellulose
    c) Chitin
    d) Protein
    Answer: d) protein (bacteria).

32. Which organelle contains DNA and protein, is limited by a nuclear membrane in eukaryotic cells, and controls various metabolic activities?
    a) Mitochondria
    b) Nucleus
    c) Chloroplast
    d) Endoplasmic reticulum
    Answer: b) Nucleus

33. Which cell function is not controlled by the nucleus?
    a) Division of the cell
    b) Inheritance of characters
    c) Site of cellular respiration
    d) Controls various metabolic activities of the cell
    Answer: c) Site of cellular respiration

34. The site of cellular respiration (Krebs cycle and electron transport chain) and association with energy release by food oxidation is the function of:
    a) Nucleus
    b) Plastids
    c) Mitochondria
    d) Ribosomes
    Answer: c) Mitochondria

35. The inner membrane of mitochondria is folded inside, forming:
    a) Stroma
    b) Grana
    c) Cristae
    d) Thylakoids
    Answer: c) cristae

36. Plastids are double membraneous structures with their inner membrane in the form of:
    a) Cristae
    b) Cisternae
    c) Thylakoids forming stroma and grana
    d) Vacuoles
    Answer: c) thylakoids forming stroma and grana

37. Which type of plastids contain chlorophyll and are the site of photosynthesis?
    a) Chromoplasts
    b) Leucoplasts
    c) Chloroplasts
    d) Amyloplasts
    Answer: c) Chloroplasts

38. Chloroplasts are often referred to as the "kitchen of a plant cell" because they are the site of:
    a) Cellular respiration
    b) Protein synthesis
    c) Photosynthesis
    d) Food storage
    Answer: c) Site of photosynthesis in green plants

39. The release of energy in the process of photosynthesis (photophosphorylation) occurs in:
    a) Mitochondria
    b) Chloroplasts
    c) Ribosomes
    d) Nucleus
    Answer: b) Chloroplasts

40. Which type of plastids contain pigments other than chlorophyll and provide different colors to flowers and fruits?
    a) Chloroplasts
    b) Leucoplasts
    c) Chromoplasts
    d) Proplastids
    Answer: c) Chromoplasts

41. Which type of plastids do not contain any pigment and store the food of the plant body?
    a) Chloroplasts
    b) Chromoplasts
    c) Leucoplasts
    d) Xanthophylls
    Answer: c) Leucoplasts

42. Which organelle consists of membrane folds forming cisternae, vesicles, and tubules, and provides a surface for various chemical reactions and substance transport within the cell?
    a) Golgi Complex
    b) Lysosomes
    c) Endoplasmic reticulum
    d) Mitochondria
    Answer: c) Endoplasmic reticulum

43. The two types of Endoplasmic Reticulum are:
    a) Rough and Smooth, depending on the presence of ribosomes
    b) Inner and Outer, depending on membrane folds
    c) Primary and Secondary
    d) Active and Inactive
    Answer: a) SER (without ribosomes) and RER (with ribosomes).

44. Which organelle consists of stacks of membranes in the form of cisternae, vacuoles, and vesicles, and is involved in secretion, absorption, and transport of substances?
    a) Endoplasmic reticulum
    b) Lysosomes
    c) Golgi Complex
    d) Mitochondria
    Answer: c) Golgi Complex

45. In plant cells, Golgi Complex is known as:
    a) Cristae
    b) Dictyosomes
    c) Thylakoids
    d) Stroma
    Answer: b) dictyosomes.

46. Single membrane sacs containing hydrolytic enzymes, often called "suicidal bags of a cell" due to digestion of own cell organelles during starvation, are called:
    a) Mitochondria
    b) Ribosomes
    c) Lysosomes
    d) Vacuoles
    Answer: c) Lysosomes

47. Peroxisomes, Spherosomes, and Glyoxysomes are types of:
    a) Large organelles
    b) Microbodies
    c) Non-membrane bound organelles
    d) Specialized vacuoles
    Answer: b) Microbodies

48. Peroxisomes are involved in:
    a) Protein synthesis
    b) Photorespiration in plant cells and lipid metabolism in animal cells
    c) Digestion
    d) Formation of spindle fibers
    Answer: b) Photorespiration in plant cells and lipid metabolism in animal cells.

49. Small spherical bodies having high deposits of fats and lipids, with enzymes like acid phosphates, are:
    a) Peroxisomes
    b) Glyoxysomes
    c) Spherosomes
    d) Lysosomes
    Answer: c) Spherosomes

50. Small bodies found in the seeds of groundnut and castor (that contain fatty acids) and convert fatty acids to soluble carbohydrates during germination are:
    a) Peroxisomes
    b) Spherosomes
    c) Glyoxysomes
    d) Lysosomes
    Answer: c) Glyoxysomes

51. Small bodies containing RNA and proteins, known as the "protein factories of a cell," are:
    a) Mitochondria
    b) Nucleus
    c) Ribosomes
    d) Golgi Complex
    Answer: c) Ribosomes

52. A group of ribosomes attached to an mRNA strand forms:
    a) Polypeptide
    b) Polyribosomes
    c) Nucleosome
    d) Chromosome
    Answer: b) polyribosomes.

53. Two dot-like bodies present near the nucleus, showing a 9+0 pattern of arrangement of microtubules, are called:
    a) Ribosomes
    b) Centriole
    c) Lysosomes
    d) Vacuoles
    Answer: b) Centriole

54. Centrioles are involved in the formation of:
    a) Cell wall
    b) Spindle-fibres at the time of cell division, cilia and flagella, and sperms from spermatids
    c) Cell membrane
    d) Plastids
    Answer: b) Forms spindle-fibres at the time of cell division. (ii) Involved in the formation of cilia and flagella of the cell. (iii) Helps in the formation of sperms from spermatids.

55. Cilia and Flagella are surrounded by a membraneous covering and show what pattern of microtubule arrangement?
    a) 9+0 pattern
    b) 9+2 pattern
    c) 2+9 pattern
    d) 0+9 pattern
    Answer: b) 9+2 pattern

56. Cilia and Flagella are primarily involved in:
    a) Photosynthesis
    b) Protein synthesis
    c) Locomotion in unicellular organisms
    d) Digestion
    Answer: c) Locomotion in unicellular organisms

57. Long, unbranched cylindrical tubes made up of proteins like tubulin and actin, forming a network in the cytoplasm, are:
    a) Lysosomes
    b) Vacuoles
    c) Microtubules and Microfilaments
    d) Ribosomes
    Answer: c) Microtubules and Microfilaments

58. Microtubules and Microfilaments are involved in:
    a) Digestion and secretion
    b) Transport of substances in higher organisms, maintenance of cell, chromosome movements, and muscle contraction/relaxation
    c) Energy production
    d) Photosynthesis
    Answer: b) Transport of substances in higher organisms. (i) Maintenance of cell and chromosome movements. (ii) Contraction and relaxation of muscles.

59. Unit membraneous bags filled with cell-fluids, with their membrane known as tonoplast, are:
    a) Lysosomes
    b) Microbodies
    c) Vacuoles
    d) Centrioles
    Answer: c) Vacuoles

60. Vacuoles contain various materials and are also:
    a) Involved in protein synthesis
    b) Osmo-regulatory in function
    c) Responsible for cell division
    d) Sites of cellular respiration
    Answer: b) Osmo-regulatory in function

Difference between Plant cell and Animal cell

    a) Smaller in size
    b) Larger in size
    c) Same size

    Answer: b) larger in size.

62. Which organelle is present in plant cells but absent in animal cells?
    a) Nucleus
    b) Mitochondria
    c) Plastid
    d) Ribosomes
    Answer: c) Plastid

63. The cell wall is present in:
    a) Animal cells only
    b) Plant cells only, made up of cellulose and chitin
    c) Both plant and animal cells
    d) Neither plant nor animal cells
    Answer: b) Plant cell present, made up of cellulose and chitin.

64. Which organelle is present in animal cells but absent in plant cells?
    a) Cell wall
    b) Plastid
    c) Centrosome
    d) Vacuoles
    Answer: c) Centrosome

65. Compared to animal cell vacuoles, plant cell vacuoles are generally:
    a) Smaller in size
    b) Larger in size
    c) Absent
    d) Identical in size
    Answer: b) larger in size.

Difference between DNA and RNA

66. The sugar found in RNA is:
    a) Deoxyribose
    b) Glucose
    c) Ribose
    d) Fructose
    Answer: c) ribose.

67. The sugar found in DNA is:
    a) Ribose
    b) Deoxyribose
    c) Glucose
    d) Fructose
    Answer: b) deoxyribose.

68. RNA is typically a:
    a) Double-stranded structure
    b) Triple-stranded structure
    c) Single-stranded structure
    d) Branched structure
    Answer: c) single-stranded structure

69. DNA is typically a:
    a) Single-stranded structure
    b) Double-stranded structure
    c) Triple-stranded structure
    d) Branched structure
    Answer: b) double-stranded structure

70. The four bases in RNA are:
    a) Adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine
    b) Adenine, guanine, uracil, cytosine
    c) Adenine, thymine, uracil, guanine
    d) Guanine, cytosine, thymine, uracil
    Answer: b) Adenine, guanine, uracil instead of thymine and cytosine are the four bases.

71. The four bases in DNA are:
    a) Adenine, guanine, uracil, cytosine
    b) Adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine
    c) Adenine, thymine, uracil, guanine
    d) Guanine, cytosine, uracil, adenine
    Answer: b) Adenine, guanine, cytosine and thymine constitute the four bases.

72. RNA is found in:
    a) Only the nucleus
    b) Only the cytoplasm
    c) Both the nucleus and in the cytoplasm
    d) Only mitochondria
    Answer: c) both in the nucleus and in the cytoplasm.

73. DNA is found in:
    a) Both the nucleus and cytoplasm
    b) Only the cytoplasm
    c) Only the nucleus and in organelles like mitochondria and chloroplasts
    d) Only ribosomes
    Answer: c) only in the nucleus and in organelles like mitochondria and chloroplasts.

Cell Division

74. In some lower animals like unicellular organisms (e.g., amoeba), cell division is a mode of:
    a) Growth
    b) Repair
    c) Reproduction
    d) Gamete formation
    Answer: c) reproduction

75. In higher organisms (plants and animals), cell division is responsible for:
    a) Only reproduction
    b) Growth, repair or replacement of injured and damaged body parts
    c) Only gamete formation
    d) Only cell differentiation
    Answer: b) growth, repair or replacement of injured and damaged body parts.

76. In sexually reproducing organisms, cell division is also involved in:
    a) Growth of body parts
    b) Repair of damaged cells
    c) Gamete formation
    d) Nutrient absorption
    Answer: c) gamete formation.

77. The two distinct modes of cell division in higher organisms are:
    a) Fission and Budding
    b) Spore formation and Fragmentation
    c) Mitosis and Meiosis
    d) Binary fission and Mitosis
    Answer: c) mitosis and meiosis.

78. Which type of cell division is an equational cell division responsible for providing growth to an organism?
    a) Meiosis
    b) Mitosis
    c) Binary fission
    d) Budding
    Answer: b) Mitosis

79. Mitosis completes in two phases:
    a) Meiosis-I and Meiosis-II
    b) Karyokinesis (nuclear division) followed by cytokinesis (cytoplasm division)
    c) Growth phase and Repair phase
    d) DNA replication and protein synthesis
    Answer: b) karyokinesis (nuclear division) followed by cytokinesis (cytoplasm division).

80. Which type of cell division is a reductional division restricted to germinal cells, involved in the formation of germ cells (gametes)?
    a) Mitosis
    b) Meiosis
    c) Karyokinesis
    d) Cytokinesis
    Answer: b) Meiosis

81. In meiosis, the daughter cells carry what number of chromosomes compared to the parental cell?
    a) Double the number (diploid)
    b) The same number (diploid)
    c) Half the number (haploid)
    d) Triple the number (triploid)
    Answer: c) half the number (haploid) chromosomes

82. The whole meiosis process completes into two broad phases called:
    a) Mitosis-I and Mitosis-II
    b) Karyokinesis and Cytokinesis
    c) Meiosis-I and Meiosis-II
    d) Prophase and Metaphase
    Answer: c) meiosis-I and meiosis-II.



BOTANY

1.  Botany is the scientific study of:
    a) Animals
    b) Fungi
    c) Plants
    d) Bacteria
    Answer: c) plants.

Classification of Plants

2.  Which type of plants do not bear flowers or seeds and reproduce by spores?
    a) Phanerogamae
    b) Angiosperms
    c) Cryptogamae
    d) Gymnosperms
    Answer: c) Cryptogamae

3.  Cryptogamae plants are divided into how many divisions?
    a) Two
    b) Three
    c) Four
    d) Five
    Answer: b) three divisions.

4.  Which are the most primitive plants, devoid of any body differentiation?
    a) Bryophytes
    b) Pteridophytes
    c) Thallophytes
    d) Gymnosperms
    Answer: c) Thallophyte

5.  Thallophytes are divided into which two classes?
    a) Mosses and Ferns
    b) Algae and Lichens
    c) Conifers and Cycads
    d) Monocotyledons and Dicotyledons
    Answer: b) Algae and Lichens

6.  Simple plants largely found in marine or freshwater habitats are:
    a) Lichens
    b) Bryophytes
    c) Algae
    d) Pteridophytes
    Answer: c) Algae

7.  An example of blue-green algae is:
    a) Ulothrix
    b) Nostoc
    c) Usnea
    d) Parmelia
    Answer: b) Nostoc

8.  Lichens are symbiotic associations between:
    a) Fungi and bacteria
    b) Algae and fungi
    c) Algae and mosses
    d) Fungi and Pteridophytes
    Answer: b) algae and fungi

9.  Lichens are considered as pioneer communities for the succession of plants on:
    a) Water surfaces
    b) Tree-trunks
    c) Rock surfaces
    d) Soil
    Answer: c) rocks surfaces.

10. Lichens are considered indicators of air pollution, particularly that caused by:
    a) Carbon dioxide
    b) Nitrogen oxides
    c) Sulfur dioxide (SO2)
    d) Methane
    Answer: c) sulphur dioxide.

11. Why are lichens considered air pollution indicators?
    a) They thrive in polluted environments.
    b) They change color in the presence of pollutants.
    c) They die or perish in the presence of SO2.
    d) They produce a special chemical in polluted air.
    Answer: c) In presence of gas SO2, they die or perish, hence indicator of air pollution.

12. Simple, terrestrial plants commonly found in moist habitats, like Funaria and Riccia, belong to which division?
    a) Thallophyte
    b) Bryophyte
    c) Pteridophyte
    d) Gymnosperms
    Answer: b) Bryophyte

13. Which plants are vascular non-seed bearing plants, found mainly in shady or damp places?
    a) Thallophytes
    b) Bryophytes
    c) Pteridophytes
    d) Angiosperms
    Answer: c) Pteridophyte

14. Which are higher plants bearing flowers and seeds, with their body differentiated into root, stem, and leaves, and a well-developed vascular system?
    a) Cryptogamae
    b) Thallophytes
    c) Phanerogamae
    d) Bryophytes
    Answer: c) Phanerogamae

15. Phanerogamae are divided into:
    a) Algae and Fungi
    b) Monocotyledons and Dicotyledons
    c) Gymnosperms and Angiosperms
    d) Bryophytes and Pteridophytes
    Answer: c) Gymnosperms and Angiosperms.

16. Which plants lack vessels and are naked seed-bearing vascular plants, including cycads and conifers?
    a) Angiosperms
    b) Pteridophytes
    c) Gymnosperms
    d) Bryophytes
    Answer: c) Gymnosperms

17. Which plants are vascular seed-bearing plants characterized by their flowers containing male and female reproductive structures?
    a) Gymnosperms
    b) Pteridophytes
    c) Angiosperms
    d) Thallophytes
    Answer: c) Angiosperms

18. Angiosperms are further divided into:
    a) Cycads and Conifers
    b) Algae and Fungi
    c) Monocotyledons and Dicotyledons
    d) Herbs and Shrubs
    Answer: c) Monocotyledons and Dicotyledons.

19. Plants characterized by parallel leaf venation, fibrous roots, and a single cotyledon in the seed are:
    a) Dicotyledons
    b) Gymnosperms
    c) Monocotyledons

20. An example of a monocotyledon is:
    a) Rose

21. Plants characterized by reticulate leaf venation, tap roots, and having two cotyledons in the seed are:
    a) Monocotyledons
    b) Gymnosperms
    c) Dicotyledons

22. An example of a dicotyledon is:
    a) Wheat
    b) Rice

    d) Rose

PLANT TISSUES

23. Growth of plants occurs only in regions where which type of tissue is present?
    a) Permanent tissue
    b) Meristematic tissue
    c) Xylem
    d) Phloem
    Answer: b) meristematic tissues

24. Meristematic tissues are classified as apical, lateral, and intercalary based on:
    a) Their function
    b) The region where they are present
    c) Their cell shape
    d) Their cell wall thickness
    Answer: b) the region where they are present

25. Which meristem is present at the growing tips of the stem and root and increases their length?
    a) Lateral meristem
    b) Intercalary meristem
    c) Apical meristem
    d) Vascular meristem
    Answer: c) Apical meristem

26. Which meristem increases the girth of the stem or root and is also called cambium?
    a) Apical meristem
    b) Intercalary meristem
    c) Lateral meristem
    d) Primary meristem
    Answer: c) Lateral meristem

27. Which meristem is located at the base of leaves or internodes on twigs?
    a) Apical meristem
    b) Lateral meristem
    c) Intercalary meristem
    d) Secondary meristem
    Answer: c) Intercalary meristem

28. Cells formed by meristematic tissue that acquire permanent shape, size, and function and lose the ability to divide form:
    a) Meristematic tissue
    b) Permanent tissue
    c) Vascular tissue
    d) Ground tissue
    Answer: b) Permanent tissue

29. Which simple permanent tissue consists of unspecialized cells with thin cell walls, loosely packed with large intercellular spaces?
    a) Collenchyma
    b) Sclerenchyma
    c) Parenchyma
    d) Xylem
    Answer: c) Parenchyma

30. When parenchyma tissue contains chlorophyll and performs photosynthesis, it is called:
    a) Aerenchyma
    b) Chlorenchyma
    c) Sclerenchyma
    d) Collenchyma
    Answer: b) chlorenchyma.

31. In aquatic plants, parenchyma with large air cavities that give buoyancy to the plants is called:
    a) Chlorenchyma
    b) Aerenchyma
    c) Collenchyma
    d) Sclerenchyma
    Answer: b) aerenchyma.

32. The functions of parenchyma include:
    a) Making the plant hard and stiff
    b) Providing flexibility
    c) Providing support to the plants and storing food and water
    d) Transporting water and minerals
    Answer: c) It provides support to the plants (ii) It stores food and water in stems and roots.

GENETICS

    a) Gregor John Mendel
    b) Watson
    c) Sutton and Boveri
    d) Har Gobind Khorana
    Answer: b) Watson

118. The three main subdivisions of genetics today are:
    a) Classical, modern, and molecular
    b) Mendelian genetics, population genetics, and biochemical genetics
    c) Plant, animal, and microbial genetics

    Answer: b) Mendelian genetics, population genetics and biochemical genetics.

119. Heredity may be defined as the transmission of characteristics from one generation to successive generations of:
    a) Non-living things
    b) Living things
    c) Chemical compounds

    Answer: b) living things.

120. The first quantitative study of inheritance was carried out by Gregor John Mendel on:
    a) Fruit flies

    Answer: b) Garden Peas (Pisum sativum).

121. A unit of heredity is called a:
    a) Chromosome
    b) Allele

    Answer: c) Gene.

122. The various forms of a gene are called:
    a) Genotypes
    b) Phenotypes
    c) Alleles
    d) Chromosomes
    Answer: c) alleles.

123. Individual organisms in which the members of a pair of alleles are different are said to be:
    a) Homozygous
    b) Heterozygous
    c) Dominant
    d) Recessive
    Answer: b) heterozygous.

124. Individuals in which the members of a pair of alleles are the same are said to be:
    a) Heterozygous
    b) Homozygous

    Answer: b) homozygous.

125. The genetic constitution of an organism is its:
    a) Phenotype

    Answer: b) Genotype.

126. The observable characteristics of an organism constitute its:
    a) Genotype
    b) Phenotype

    Answer: b) Phenotype.

127. The ability of a gene to have many effects is called:
    a) Codominance
    b) Incomplete dominance
    c) Pleiotropy
    d) Epistasis
    Answer: c) Pleiotropy.

128. A system to analyze the distribution and movement of traits in the family tree is called:
    a) Karyotyping
    b) Gene mapping
    c) Pedigree analysis
    d) DNA sequencing
    Answer: c) Pedigree analysis

129. The Chromosome Theory of Inheritance, proposed by Sutton and Boveri, states that Mendelian genes are located on __________ and it is the __________ that segregate and assort independently.
    a) Alleles, genes
    b) Proteins, enzymes
    c) Chromosomes, chromosomes
    d) Traits, phenotypes
    Answer: c) chromosomes, and it is the chromosomes that segregate and assort independently.

130. Sexually reproducing animals have a set of chromosomes called autosomes and a pair of:
    a) Mitochondrial chromosomes
    b) Plastid chromosomes

131. In human females, there are:
    a) One X and one Y chromosome
    b) Two Y chromosomes

    d) No sex chromosomes
    Answer: c) two X chromosomes

132. In human males, there are:
    a) Two X chromosomes
    b) Two Y chromosomes
    c) One X and one Y Chromosome
    d) No sex chromosomes
    Answer: c) one X and one Y Chromosome.

133. Har Gobind Khorana, along with his associates, manufactured short DNA molecules having a known sequence of bases, which he called a:
    a) Protein
    b) RNA
    c) Gene
    d) Chromosome
    Answer: c) Gene.

134. For his work on directing protein synthesis by RNA molecules with known base sequences, Har Gobind Khorana shared the 1968 Nobel Prize in Medicine with:
    a) Watson and Crick
    b) M.W. Nirenberg and R.H. Holley
    c) Sutton and Boveri
    d) Mendel and Darwin
    Answer: b) M.W. Nirenberg and R.H. Holley (1968) Nobel prize in medicine.

135. The Central Dogma of molecular biology, as described, is:
    a) Protein → mRNA → DNA
    b) DNA → Translation → mRNA → Transcription → Protein

Genetic Disorders

136. Albinism is caused by a recessive gene that blocks the conversion of amino acid tyrosine to:
    a) Haemoglobin
    b) Collagen
    c) Melanin
    d) Keratin
    Answer: c) melanin.

137. The effect of albinism is the absence of pigment in:
    a) Blood and bones
    b) Skin, hair, and iris of eyes
    c) Internal organs
    d) Muscles
    Answer: b) skin, hair and iris of eyes.

138. Down syndrome (Mongolism) is caused by:
    a) Monosomy of chromosome 21
    b) Trisomy of chromosome 21
    c) Deletion of a sex chromosome
    d) Duplication of a sex chromosome
    Answer: b) trisomy of chromosome 21.

139. The effect of Down syndrome is:
    a) Normal physical and mental development
    b) Physically and mentally retarded patient with a round face and widely separated eyes
    c) Muscle weakness and atrophy
    d) Defective blood clotting
    Answer: b) physically and mentally retarded patient with round face and widely separated eyes.

140. Galactosemia is caused by a recessive autosomal gene that results in a defect in the enzyme system utilizing sugar galactose, leading to:
    a) Low levels of glucose in blood
    b) High levels of galactose in blood, resulting in cataract and brain damage
    c) Absence of melanin
    d) Abnormal hemoglobin molecules
    Answer: b) high–level of galactose in blood; resulted in development of cataract and brain damage.

141. Hemophilia is caused by a sex-linked recessive gene that produces a defective protein essential for:
    a) Oxygen transport
    b) Blood clotting
    c) Muscle contraction
    d) Hormone synthesis
    Answer: b) clotting of blood.

142. Huntington's disease is caused by a dominant autosomal gene that leads to progressive deterioration of the:
    a) Muscular system
    b) Digestive system
    c) Nervous system
    d) Skeletal system
    Answer: c) nervous system.

143. A peculiar feature of Huntington's disease is that symptoms typically develop between:
    a) 1-10 years
    b) 10-20 years
    c) 30 to 40 years
    d) 50-60 years
    Answer: c) 30 to 40 years.

144. Klinefelter syndrome is caused by an additional:
    a) Y-chromosome
    b) X-chromosome
    c) Autosome
    d) Deletion of a chromosome
    Answer: b) X-chromosome.

145. Klinefelter syndrome results in:
    a) Normal development of testes
    b) Improper development of testes
    c) Rudimentary sex organs in females
    d) Muscle weakness
    Answer: b) improper development of testes.

146. Muscular Dystrophy is caused by a sex-linked recessive gene that results in:
    a) Bone fragility
    b) Childhood muscle weakness suffering and atrophy
    c) Brain damage and intellectual disability
    d) Vision impairment
    Answer: b) childhood muscle weakness suffering and atrophy.

147. Phenylketonuria (PKU) is caused by a recessive autosomal gene in a homozygous condition, resulting in the accumulation of acid phenylalanine and:
    a) Muscle atrophy
    b) Hearing loss
    c) Brain damage
    d) Cataract formation
    Answer: c) brain damage.

148. Phenylketonuria (PKU) can be prevented by:
    a) Gene therapy
    b) Surgical intervention
    c) Giving a phenylalanine diet
    d) Vitamin supplements
    Answer: c) giving phenylalanine diet.

149. Sickle Cell Anaemia is caused by a recessive gene that leads to:
    a) Overproduction of red blood cells
    b) Abnormal haemoglobin molecules, making RBCs sickle-shaped with reduced haemoglobin
    c) Excessive blood clotting
    d) Absence of pigment
    Answer: b) abnormal haemoglobin molecule. RBC are sickle shaped with reduced haemoglobin.

150. Tay-Sach disease is caused by an autosomal recessive gene, resulting in:
    a) Muscle weakness
    b) Cerebral degeneration, epilepsy, paralysis, and blindness
    c) Defective blood clotting
    d) Abnormal hemoglobin structure
    Answer: b) cerebral degeneration, epilepsy, paralysis and blindness.

151. Thalassemia (Cooley’s anaemia) is mostly seen in children and caused by a recessive gene, resulting in:
    a) Sickle-shaped RBCs
    b) Abnormality in haemoglobin structures
    c) Progressive deterioration of the nervous system
    d) Absence of pigment
    Answer: b) abnormality in haemoglobin structures.

152. Turner Syndrome is caused by a lack of:
    a) Y chromosome (XY)
    b) X chromosome (OX)
    c) An autosome
    d) A pair of chromosomes
    Answer: b) X chromosome (OX).

153. Turner Syndrome results in:
    a) Phenotypical male with improper testes development
    b) Phenotypical female but with rudimentary sex organs and mammary glands
    c) Mental retardation and widely separated eyes
    d) Muscle weakness and atrophy
    Answer: b) phenotypical female but have rudimentary sex organ and mammary gland.
