

PHYSICS - GENERAL

1.  Physics is the branch of science that deals with the study of:
    a) Matter and its composition
    b) Energy and its transformations
    c) Matter and energy and their inter-relationship
    d) Living organisms
    Answer: c) Matter and energy and their inter-relationship

---

MOTION

2.  Motion is defined as a change in position of a body with respect to:
    a) Only time
    b) Only a reference point
    c) Time and a reference point
    d) Force applied
    Answer: c) Time and a reference point

3.  Which of the following is NOT a type of motion mentioned?
    a) Linear Motion
    b) Curvilinear Motion
    c) Rotatory Motion
    d) Oscillatory Motion
    Answer: d) Oscillatory Motion

---

SCALAR AND VECTOR QUANTITIES

4.  Which type of physical quantities are completely described by their magnitude alone?
    a) Vector quantities
    b) Scalar quantities
    c) Both scalar and vector quantities
    d) Neither scalar nor vector quantities
    Answer: b) Scalar quantities

5.  Which of the following is an example of a scalar quantity?
    a) Displacement
    b) Velocity
    c) Force
    d) Work
    Answer: d) Work

6.  Which of the following is an example of a vector quantity?
    a) Length
    b) Mass
    c) Temperature
    d) Momentum
    Answer: d) Momentum

7.  Which physical quantity requires both magnitude and direction for its complete description?
    a) Area
    b) Volume
    c) Pressure
    d) Acceleration
    Answer: d) Acceleration

DISTANCE

8.  Distance traveled by a body is described as the:
    a) Shortest path between initial and final position.
    b) Actual length of the path covered irrespective of direction.
    c) Rate of change of displacement.
    d) Displacement per unit time.
    Answer: b) Actual length of the path covered irrespective of direction.

9.  Distance is a:
    a) Vector quantity
    b) Scalar quantity
    c) Dimensionless quantity
    d) Fundamental quantity
    Answer: b) Scalar quantity

---

DISPLACEMENT

10. Displacement is defined as the:
    a) Actual length of the path covered.
    b) Distance traveled in one second.
    c) Shortest distance between the initial and final position along with direction.
    d) Rate of change of velocity.
    Answer: c) Shortest distance between the initial and final position along with direction.

11. Displacement is a:
    a) Scalar quantity
    b) Vector quantity
    c) Unitless quantity
    d) Pure number
    Answer: b) Vector quantity

SPEED

12. Speed is the scalar form of:
    a) Displacement
    b) Acceleration
    c) Velocity
    d) Momentum
    Answer: c) Velocity

13. Speed is defined as the:
    a) Displacement traveled in one second.
    b) Distance traveled in one second.
    c) Rate of change of displacement.
    d) Rate of change of velocity.
    Answer: b) Distance traveled in one second.

14. If a body covers a distance 'd' in time 't', the speed (V) is given by the formula:
    a) V = t/d
    b) V = d/t
    c) V = d  t
    d) V = d + t
    Answer: b) V = d/t

AVERAGE SPEED

15. If a body has travelled 'S1' and 'S2' distance with 'V1' and 'V2' velocities, the average speed is given by:
    a) (V1 + V2) / 2
    b) (S1 + S2) / (V1 + V2)
    c) Total distance / Total time
    d) (S1  V1) + (S2  V2)
    Answer: c) Total distance / Total time

VELOCITY

16. The rate of change of displacement is called:
    a) Speed
    b) Acceleration
    c) Velocity
    d) Momentum
    Answer: c) Velocity

17. Velocity is a:
    a) Scalar quantity
    b) Vector quantity
    c) Constant quantity
    d) Pure number
    Answer: b) Vector quantity

18. Velocity can be:
    a) Only positive
    b) Only negative
    c) Only zero
    d) Positive, negative, or zero depending on displacement
    Answer: d) Positive, negative, or zero depending on displacement.

---

UNIFORM VELOCITY

19. When a body covers equal displacement in equal intervals of time, the velocity is said to be:
    a) Variable
    b) Average
    c) Uniform
    d) Instantaneous
    Answer: c) Uniform

20. If a body moves with uniform velocity, which of the following is true?
    a) Acceleration is necessarily present.
    b) Initial velocity is greater than final velocity.
    c) Acceleration is absent.
    d) Direction of velocity changes.
    Answer: c) Acceleration is absent.

---

VARIABLE VELOCITY

21. When a body covers unequal displacement in equal intervals of time, it is said to have:
    a) Uniform velocity
    b) Constant speed
    c) Variable velocity
    d) Zero acceleration
    Answer: c) Variable velocity

22. In motion with variable velocity, what is necessarily present?
    a) Uniform speed
    b) Zero acceleration
    c) Acceleration
    d) Constant displacement
    Answer: c) Acceleration

23. In uniform circular motion, what changes with respect to time, even if speed is constant?
    a) Magnitude of velocity
    b) Direction of velocity
    c) Both magnitude and direction of velocity
    d) Displacement becomes zero
    Answer: b) Direction of velocity

---

AVERAGE VELOCITY

24. Average velocity is zero if the body:
    a) Moves with constant speed.
    b) Moves in a straight line.
    c) Returns to its starting point in the given time interval.
    d) Covers unequal displacement.
    Answer: c) Returns to its starting point in the given time interval.

25. A body whose velocity reduces with the passage of time is said to be undergoing:
    a) Acceleration
    b) Deceleration or retardation
    c) Uniform motion
    d) Constant velocity
    Answer: b) Deceleration or retardation

26. The acceleration of a moving body is the rate of change of its:
    a) Displacement
    b) Speed
    c) Velocity
    d) Position
    Answer: c) Velocity

ACCELERATION

27. Time rate of change in velocity is known as:
    a) Speed
    b) Displacement
    c) Acceleration
    d) Force
    Answer: c) Acceleration

28. Acceleration is a:
    a) Scalar quantity
    b) Vector quantity
    c) Dimensionless quantity
    d) Pure number
    Answer: b) Vector quantity

29. Negative acceleration is known as:
    a) Acceleration due to gravity
    b) Impulse
    c) Retardation
    d) Uniform acceleration
    Answer: c) Retardation

30. What does negative acceleration indicate?
    a) The velocity of the object is increasing.
    b) The velocity of the object is decreasing with respect to time.
    c) The object is at rest.
    d) The object is moving with constant velocity.
    Answer: b) The velocity of the object is decreasing with respect to time.

---

VARIABLE ACCELERATION

31. If the velocity of a body changes in different amounts during the same time intervals, its acceleration is known as:
    a) Uniform acceleration
    b) Constant acceleration
    c) Variable acceleration
    d) Zero acceleration
    Answer: c) Variable acceleration

32. Acceleration is variable if:
    a) Only its direction changes.
    b) Only its magnitude changes.
    c) Both its direction and magnitude change.
    d) Either its direction or magnitude or both changes with respect to time.
    Answer: d) Either its direction or magnitude or both changes with respect to time.

33. A body in uniform circular motion is an example of motion with:
    a) Zero acceleration
    b) Uniform acceleration
    c) Variable acceleration
    d) Constant speed and constant velocity
    Answer: c) Variable acceleration

---

UNIFORM ACCELERATION

34. If the velocity of the body changes by the same amounts in the same intervals of time, then the acceleration of the body is known as:
    a) Variable acceleration
    b) Non-uniform acceleration
    c) Uniform acceleration
    d) Deceleration
    Answer: c) Uniform acceleration

35. What is an example of uniform acceleration?
    a) A motor cyclist riding on a busy city road.
    b) Acceleration due to gravity.
    c) A car speeding up randomly.
    d) A ball rolling on an uneven surface.
    Answer: b) Acceleration due to gravity.

UNIFORM CIRCULAR MOTION

36. When a particle moves in a circle at a constant speed, the motion is called:
    a) Linear motion
    b) Rotatory motion
    c) Uniform circular motion
    d) Vibratory motion
    Answer: c) Uniform circular motion

37. Why is circular motion considered as accelerated motion, even at constant speed?
    a) Because its speed changes.
    b) Because its position and thus velocity direction change.
    c) Because it covers equal displacement in equal time.
    d) Because it is under gravitational influence.
    Answer: b) Because its position and thus velocity direction change.

38. In circular motion, the acceleration which is always directed towards the center of the circle is called:
    a) Tangential acceleration
    b) Centripetal acceleration
    c) Gravitational acceleration
    d) Linear acceleration
    Answer: b) Centripetal acceleration

EQUATIONS OF MOTION

39. In the equation $v = u + at$, what does 'u' represent?
    a) Final velocity
    b) Initial velocity
    c) Acceleration
    d) Time
    Answer: b) Initial velocity

40. In the equation $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$, what does 's' represent?
    a) Speed
    b) Elapsed time
    c) Acceleration
    d) Distance/displacement covered
    Answer: d) Distance/displacement covered

41. What is the mean value of acceleration due to gravity ('g')?
    a) 9.8 m/s
    b) 9.8 km/s$^2$
    c) 9.8 m/s$^2$
    d) 9.8 km/s
    Answer: c) 9.8 m/s$^2$

42. If a body travels downwards, what is the sign of 'g'?
    a) Negative
    b) Zero
    c) Positive
    d) Variable
    Answer: c) Positive

43. If a body is projected vertically upwards, what is the sign of 'g'?
    a) Positive
    b) Negative
    c) Zero
    d) Constant
    Answer: b) Negative

LAWS OF MOTION

44. Newton’s First Law of Motion is also called the:
    a) Law of acceleration
    b) Law of action and reaction
    c) Law of Inertia
    d) Law of conservation of momentum
    Answer: c) Law of Inertia

45. According to Newton's Second Law of Motion, the rate of change of momentum is directly proportional to:
    a) Applied displacement.
    b) Applied velocity.
    c) Applied force.
    d) Applied acceleration.
    Answer: c) Applied force.

46. Newton's Second Law of Motion can also be termed as the:
    a) Law of Inertia
    b) Law of acceleration
    c) Law of conservation of energy
    d) Law of reaction
    Answer: b) Law of acceleration

47. Newton's Third Law of Motion states that:
    a) Every body continues in a state of rest or uniform motion.
    b) The rate of change of momentum is proportional to applied force.
    c) For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
    d) Force equals mass times acceleration.
    Answer: c) For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

48. According to Newton's Third Law, on how many different bodies do the forces act?
    a) One
    b) Two
    c) Three
    d) Four
    Answer: b) Two

FORCE AND INERTIA

49. What is a force defined as?
    a) Only a pull.
    b) Only a push.
    c) A pull or push that changes or tends to change the state of motion or shape of a body.
    d) The mass of an object.
    Answer: c) A pull or push that changes or tends to change the state of motion or shape of a body.

50. The tendency of a body to remain in the same state (either stationary or moving) is called its:
    a) Momentum
    b) Acceleration
    c) Inertia
    d) Weight
    Answer: c) Inertia

51. What is the relationship between mass and inertia?
    a) Less mass means greater inertia.
    b) Mass and inertia are unrelated.
    c) The more the mass of the objects, the greater is their inertia.
    d) Inertia is only dependent on velocity.
    Answer: c) The more the mass of the objects, the greater is their inertia.

52. The force (F) which causes an acceleration (a) in a moving object of mass (m) is given by:
    a) F = m/a
    b) F = a/m
    c) F = ma
    d) F = m + a
    Answer: c) F = ma

53. What is the SI unit of force?
    a) Joule
    b) Watt
    c) Newton
    d) Pascal
    Answer: c) Newton (kg.ms$^{-2}$)

BANKING OF TRACKS

54. Why are roads on large highways generally banked at curved paths?
    a) To increase friction.
    b) To reduce the speed of vehicles.
    c) To supply the necessary centripetal force for circular motion.
    d) To make the road flatter.

    a) Increases
    b) Decreases
    c) Remains conserved
    d) Becomes zero
    Answer: c) Remains conserved.

    a) Lighter
    b) Heavier

    d) Unchanged
    Answer: b) Heavier

72. When an elevator is accelerating downward, how does one feel?
    a) Heavier
    b) Lighter
    c) Weightless
    d) Unchanged
    Answer: b) Lighter

    a) The elevator stops completely.
    b) Both elevator and the person accelerate downwards at the same rate.

FRICTION

74. Friction is a kind of force that:
    a) Promotes motion.
    b) Opposes relative motion.
    c) Acts only on smooth surfaces.
    d) Depends on the area of contact.
    Answer: b) Opposes relative motion.

75. Force of friction does NOT depend on:
    a) The area of contact.
    b) The weight of the bodies.
    c) The roughness of the surfaces.
    d) The nature of surfaces in contact.
    Answer: a) The area of contact.

76. Frictional force increases with:
    a) Temperature
    b) Speed
    c) Weight
    d) Smoothness
    Answer: c) Weight

77. On rough surfaces, frictional force is:
    a) Less than on smooth surfaces.
    b) The same as on smooth surfaces.
    c) More than on smooth surfaces.
    d) Zero.
    Answer: c) More than on smooth surfaces.

78. Which type of friction is less than static friction, meaning less force is needed to maintain motion than to initiate it?
    a) Rolling friction
    b) Kinetic friction
    c) Fluid friction
    d) Air resistance
    Answer: b) Kinetic friction

79. Why are vehicles provided with wheels and axles supported with ball bearings?
    a) To increase static friction.
    b) Because rolling friction is much less than sliding friction.
    c) To make the vehicle heavier.
    d) To reduce kinetic energy.
    Answer: b) Because the rolling friction is much less than the sliding friction.

80. Why is friction considered a "necessary evil"?
    a) Because it always hinders motion.
    b) Because it is only present in some situations.
    c) Because it is necessary for operations like brakes and for things like nails to stay fixed, but also opposes motion.
    d) Because it generates heat.
    Answer: c) Because it is necessary for operations like brakes and for things like nails to stay fixed, but also opposes motion.

WORK, POWER AND ENERGY

81. Work is said to be done only when a force or its component causes:
    a) Change in temperature.
    b) Displacement in its own direction, stops a body, or changes its shape.
    c) Increase in mass.
    d) Decrease in velocity.
    Answer: b) Displacement in its own direction, stops a body, or changes its shape.

82. What is the SI unit of work?
    a) Watt
    b) Newton
    c) Joule
    d) Pascal
    Answer: c) Joule (kg m$^2$s$^{-2}$)

83. The formula for work (W) is:
    a) W = F + d
    b) W = F / d
    c) W = F × d
    d) W = F - d
    Answer: c) W = F × d

84. What is the rate of doing work called?
    a) Energy
    b) Force
    c) Momentum
    d) Power
    Answer: d) Power

85. Power (P) can be defined as the product of:
    a) Mass and acceleration.
    b) Force and displacement.
    c) Force and velocity.
    d) Work and time.
    Answer: c) Force and velocity.

86. What is the SI unit of power?
    a) Joule/second or Watt
    b) Newton
    c) Pascal
    d) Kilogram
    Answer: a) Joule/second (JS$^{-1}$) or Watt

87. What is the capacity of a body to do work called?
    a) Power
    b) Force
    c) Energy
    d) Momentum
    Answer: c) Energy

88. The unit of energy is the same as that of:
    a) Power
    b) Force
    c) Work
    d) Pressure
    Answer: c) Work (Joule)

89. When a body does work by virtue of its motion, it is said to have:
    a) Potential energy
    b) Chemical energy
    c) Kinetic energy
    d) Thermal energy
    Answer: c) Kinetic energy

90. The formula for Kinetic Energy (K.E.) of a body with mass 'm' and velocity 'v' is:
    a) K.E. = mv
    b) K.E. = mgh
    c) K.E. = 1/2 mv$^2$
    d) K.E. = mv$^2$
    Answer: c) K.E. = 1/2 mv$^2$

91. If a body of mass 'm' is raised vertically through a height 'h' against acceleration due to gravity 'g', its Potential Energy (P.E.) is given by:
    a) P.E. = 1/2 mv$^2$
    b) P.E. = mg/h
    c) P.E. = mgh
    d) P.E. = gh/m
    Answer: c) P.E. = mgh

92. When a body is on the surface of Earth, the potential energy between the system of the body and the Earth is:
    a) Maximum
    b) Minimum
    c) Zero
    d) Dependent on its mass
    Answer: c) Zero

93. Telecommunication satellites, also called geostationary satellites, are placed at what approximate height from the Earth's surface?
    a) 800 km

    c) 36,000 km
    d) 80,000 km

SURFACE TENSION

    a) Expand and occupy maximum surface area.
    b) Contract and occupy the minimum possible surface area.
    c) Become more viscous.
    d) Change its temperature.

    a) Increases.
    b) Decreases.

109. The addition of which type of impurity would increase surface tension if it is soluble in water?
    a) Soap

    Answer: c) Common salt or sugar

110. The surface tension of liquid metals like mercury is:
    a) Very low.
