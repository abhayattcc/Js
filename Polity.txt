

POLITY AND CONSTITUTION

Evolution of the Constitution of India

1.  A constitution is described as a document of people's faith and aspirations possessing a special legal sanctity. It is considered the:
    a) Secondary law of the country
    b) Fundamental law of the country
    c) Temporary law of the country
    d) Customary law of the country
    Answer: b) Fundamental law of the country

THE REGULATING ACT, 1773

2.  The Regulating Act of 1773 provided for the centralization of administration of the company's territories in:
    a) Bombay
    b) Madras
    c) India
    d) Calcutta
    Answer: c) India

3.  According to the Regulating Act of 1773, the Governor of Bengal became the:
    a) Governor of Calcutta
    b) Governor-General of India
    c) Governor-General of Bengal
    d) Viceroy of India
    Answer: c) Governor-General of Bengal

4.  A Supreme Court was to be set up at Calcutta in 1774 as per which Act?
    a) Pitt's India Act, 1784
    b) The Regulating Act, 1773
    c) The Charter Act, 1793
    d) The Charter Act, 1813
    Answer: b) The Regulating Act, 1773

THE PITT’S INDIA ACT, 1784

5.  The Pitt's India Act of 1784 subordinated the Bombay and Madras Presidencies to the Bengal Presidency in matters of:
    a) Trade and Commerce
    b) War, diplomacy and revenues
    c) Administration and Justice
    d) Education and Social Reforms
    Answer: b) War, diplomacy and revenues

6.  Under the Pitt's India Act, 1784, the strength of the Governor-General in council was reduced to:
    a) 2
    b) 3
    c) 4
    d) 5
    Answer: b) 3

7.  Which Act distinguished between the commercial and political functions of the company and established a system of double government?
    a) The Regulating Act, 1773
    b) The Pitt's India Act, 1784
    c) The Charter Act, 1793
    d) The Charter Act, 1813
    Answer: b) The Pitt's India Act, 1784

8.  The British Government was given supreme control over the company's affairs and its administration in India by which Act?
    a) The Charter Act, 1833
    b) The Charter Act, 1853
    c) The Pitt's India Act, 1784
    d) The Government of India Act, 1858
    Answer: c) The Pitt's India Act, 1784

THE CHARTER ACT, 1793

9.  Which Act gave the power to the Governor-General to override his council?
    a) The Regulating Act, 1773
    b) The Pitt's India Act, 1784
    c) The Charter Act, 1793
    d) The Charter Act, 1813
    Answer: c) The Charter Act, 1793

THE CHARTER ACT, 1813

10. The Act that provided rules and procedures for the use of Indian revenue was:
    a) The Charter Act, 1793
    b) The Charter Act, 1813
    c) The Charter Act, 1833
    d) The Charter Act, 1853
    Answer: b) The Charter Act, 1813

THE CHARTER ACT, 1833

11. The Governor-General of Bengal was designated as the Governor-General of India by which Act?
    a) The Charter Act, 1813
    b) The Charter Act, 1833
    c) The Charter Act, 1853
    d) The Government of India Act, 1858
    Answer: b) The Charter Act, 1833

12. Who was the first Governor-General of India?
    a) Lord William Bentinck
    b) Lord Dalhousie
    c) Lord Canning
    d) Lord Minto
    Answer: a) Lord William Bentinck

13. Which presidencies were deprived of legislative powers by the Charter Act of 1833?
    a) Calcutta and Bombay
    b) Madras and Bengal
    c) Madras and Bombay
    d) Bengal and Calcutta
    Answer: c) Madras and Bombay

14. A law member was added to the council of the Governor-General under which Act?
    a) The Charter Act, 1793
    b) The Charter Act, 1813
    c) The Charter Act, 1833
    d) The Charter Act, 1853
    Answer: c) The Charter Act, 1833

THE CHARTER ACT, 1853

15. For the first time, a separate legislative machinery consisting of a 12-member legislative council, known as the Indian (central) legislative council, was created by which Act?
    a) The Charter Act, 1833
    b) The Charter Act, 1853
    c) The Government of India Act, 1858
    d) The Indian Council Act, 1861
    Answer: b) The Charter Act, 1853

16. Which Act introduced an open competition system of selection and recruitment of civil servants?
    a) The Charter Act, 1813
    b) The Charter Act, 1833
    c) The Charter Act, 1853
    d) The Government of India Act, 1858
    Answer: c) The Charter Act, 1853

17. Under the Charter Act of 1853, the law member was made a full member of the:
    a) Legislative Council
    b) Judicial Council
    c) Executive Council of the Governor-General
    d) Board of Control
    Answer: c) Executive Council of the Governor-General

THE GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1858

18. The Government of India Act, 1858, created a new office of:
    a) Governor-General of India
    b) Viceroy of India
    c) Secretary of State for India
    d) Commander-in-Chief
    Answer: c) Secretary of State for India

19. The Secretary of State for India, created by the Government of India Act, 1858, was assisted by a:
    a) 10-member council
    b) 12-member council
    c) 15-member council
    d) 20-member council
    Answer: c) 15-member council

20. After the Government of India Act, 1858, the Governor-General was to be called as the:
    a) President of India
    b) Crown Representative
    c) Viceroy
    d) Imperial Governor
    Answer: c) Viceroy

21. Which Act ended the system of double government by abolishing the Board of Control and Court of Directors?
    a) The Charter Act, 1853
    b) The Government of India Act, 1858
    c) The Indian Council Act, 1861
    d) The Indian Council Act, 1892
    Answer: b) The Government of India Act, 1858

22. The Act known as the Act for the Good Government of India, which abolished the East India Company and transferred powers to the British Crown, is:
    a) The Charter Act, 1833
    b) The Charter Act, 1853
    c) The Government of India Act, 1858
    d) The Indian Council Act, 1861
    Answer: c) The Government of India Act, 1858

THE INDIAN COUNCIL ACT, 1861

23. Which Act marked the beginning of associating Indians in legislation?
    a) The Government of India Act, 1858
    b) The Indian Council Act, 1861
    c) The Indian Council Act, 1892
    d) The Morley-Minto Reforms, 1909
    Answer: b) The Indian Council Act, 1861

24. Under the Indian Council Act, 1861, a fifth member was added to the:
    a) Legislative Council
    b) Viceroy's Executive Council
    c) Secretary of State's Council
    d) Board of Control
    Answer: b) Viceroy's Executive Council

25. For legislative purposes, the executive council of the Viceroy was enlarged by how many members according to the Indian Council Act, 1861?
    a) 3 to 6 members
    b) 4 to 8 members
    c) 6 to 12 members
    d) 8 to 15 members
    Answer: c) 6 to 12 members

THE INDIAN COUNCIL ACT, 1892

26. Which Act introduced indirect elections for the non-official members of the Imperial and Provincial Legislative Councils?
    a) The Indian Council Act, 1861
    b) The Indian Council Act, 1892
    c) The Morley-Minto Reforms, 1909
    d) The Government of India Act, 1919
    Answer: b) The Indian Council Act, 1892

27. According to the Indian Council Act, 1892, the councils at both levels were given the power of:
    a) Voting on the Budget
    b) Discussing the Budget but not voting
    c) Introducing new laws
    d) Appointing officials
    Answer: b) Discussing the Budget but not voting

THE MORLEY-MINTO REFORMS (THE INDIAN COUNCIL ACT, 1909)

28. The Morley-Minto Reforms introduced a provision for:
    a) Direct elections to legislative councils
    b) Indirect elections to legislative councils
    c) Nomination of all members
    d) Universal adult suffrage
    Answer: b) Indirect elections to legislative councils

29. Under the Morley-Minto Reforms, the additional members in the Central Legislative Council were increased to:
    a) 40
    b) 50
    c) 60
    d) 70
    Answer: c) 60

30. The Morley-Minto Reforms gave members of the Legislative Councils the right of:
    a) Vetoing legislation
    b) Asking supplementary questions
    c) Impeaching officials
    d) Introducing private bills
    Answer: b) Asking supplementary questions

31. Which Act introduced a system of communal representation for Muslims by accepting the concept of "Separate electorate"?
    a) The Indian Council Act, 1892
    b) The Morley-Minto Reforms, 1909
    c) The Government of India Act, 1919
    d) The Government of India Act, 1935
    Answer: b) The Morley-Minto Reforms, 1909

32. Who is known as the "Father of Communal Electorate"?
    a) Lord Curzon
    b) Lord Minto
    c) Lord Morley
    d) Lord Hardinge
    Answer: b) Lord Minto

THE GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1919 (MONTAGUE-CHELMSFORD REFORMS)

33. Which system was introduced in the provinces by the Government of India Act, 1919?
    a) Federalism
    b) Unitary system
    c) Dyarchy
    d) Provincial Autonomy
    Answer: c) Dyarchy

34. Under the Montague-Chelmsford Reforms, the Provincial subjects of administration were divided into:
    a) Central and Provincial
    b) Legislative and Executive
    c) 'Transferred' and 'Reserved' Subjects
    d) General and Specific
    Answer: c) 'Transferred' and 'Reserved' Subjects

35. The Indian legislature became 'bicameral' under which Act?
    a) The Morley-Minto Reforms, 1909
    b) The Government of India Act, 1919
    c) The Government of India Act, 1935
    d) The Indian Independence Act, 1947
    Answer: b) The Government of India Act, 1919

36. Communal representation was extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans by which Act?
    a) The Indian Council Act, 1909
    b) The Government of India Act, 1919
    c) The Government of India Act, 1935
    d) The Indian Independence Act, 1947
    Answer: b) The Government of India Act, 1919

37. Which Act provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission?
    a) The Indian Council Act, 1909
    b) The Government of India Act, 1919
    c) The Government of India Act, 1935
    d) The Indian Independence Act, 1947
    Answer: b) The Government of India Act, 1919

38. For the first time, provincial budgets were separated from the Central budget by which Act?
    a) The Indian Council Act, 1909
    b) The Government of India Act, 1919
    c) The Government of India Act, 1935
    d) The Indian Independence Act, 1947
    Answer: b) The Government of India Act, 1919

IMPORTANT FACTS

73. The Constitution of India was formally enacted on:
    a) 26 January, 1950
    b) 26 November, 1949
    c) 15 August, 1947
    d) 9 December, 1946
    Answer: b) 26 November, 1949

74. The Constitution of India came into force on:
    a) 26 November, 1949
    b) 15 August, 1947
    c) 26 January, 1950
    d) 9 December, 1946
    Answer: c) 26 January, 1950

75. How many Articles and Schedules were in the Constitution when it was finally passed on November 26, 1949?
    a) 395 Articles and 8 Schedules
    b) 444 Articles and 12 Schedules
    c) 390 Articles and 7 Schedules
    d) 400 Articles and 10 Schedules
    Answer: a) 395 Articles and 8 Schedules

76. At present, how many Articles and Schedules does the Constitution of India consist of?
    a) 395 Articles and 8 Schedules
    b) 444 Articles and 12 Schedules
    c) 450 Articles and 10 Schedules
    d) 470 Articles and 15 Schedules
    Answer: b) 444 Articles and 12 Schedules

77. The Constitution of India was framed and adopted by the:
    a) Parliament of India
    b) British Parliament
    c) Constituent Assembly of India
    d) Cabinet Mission
    Answer: c) Constituent Assembly of India

78. As per the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946, the Constituent Assembly was set up in:
    a) October 1946
    b) November 1946
    c) December 1946
    d) January 1947
