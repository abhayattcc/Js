

ECONOMY AND ECONOMICS

1.  What is the primary focus of "Economy"?
    a) The branch of knowledge concerned with wealth transfer.
    b) The state of a country in terms of production, consumption, and money supply.
    c) The study of individual market decisions.
    d) The analysis of global economic systems.
    Answer: b) The state of a country in terms of production, consumption, and money supply.

2.  Which statement best defines "Economics"?
    a) The production and consumption of goods and services.
    b) The supply of money in a region.
    c) The branch of knowledge concerned with the production, consumption, and transfer of wealth.
    d) The study of government intervention in markets.
    Answer: c) The branch of knowledge concerned with the production, consumption, and transfer of wealth.

VARIOUS TYPES OF ECONOMY

3.  Which of the following is a key feature of a Capitalist Economy?
    a) Social ownership of the means of production.
    b) Direct public ownership.
    c) Private ownership of capital and production inputs.
    d) Production carried out directly for use.
    Answer: c) Private ownership of capital and production inputs.

4.  In a Capitalist Economy, what mechanism primarily determines prices?
    a) Government decree
    b) Democratic control
    c) Price Mechanism
    d) Central planning
    Answer: c) Price Mechanism

5.  What is a defining characteristic of a Socialist Economy?
    a) Dominance of the profit motive.
    b) Private property rights.
    c) Social ownership and democratic control of the means of production.
    d) Absence of government interference.
    Answer: c) Social ownership and democratic control of the means of production.

6.  An economic system that combines elements of markets and economic planning, or public and private ownership, is known as a:
    a) Capitalist Economy
    b) Socialist Economy
    c) Mixed Economy
    d) Closed Economy
    Answer: c) Mixed Economy

7.  Which type of economy is the Indian economy cited as an example of?
    a) Capitalist Economy
    b) Socialist Economy
    c) Mixed Economy
    d) Open Economy
    Answer: c) Mixed Economy

8.  An economy generally free from trade barriers where exports and imports form a large percentage of GDP is called an:
    a) Closed Economy
    b) Mixed Economy
    c) Open Economy
    d) Socialist Economy
    Answer: c) Open Economy

9.  What is the primary characteristic of a Closed Economy?
    a) High levels of international trade.
    b) No economic activity conducted with other economies.
    c) A balance between public and private ownership.
    d) Reliance on price mechanisms.
    Answer: b) No economic activity conducted with other economies.

ECONOMIC GROWTH

10. What is Economic Growth defined as?
    a) A decrease in the production of goods and services.
    b) An increase in the amount of goods and services produced per head of the population over a period of time.
    c) A decline in aggregate demand.
    d) A reduction in productive capacity.
    Answer: b) An increase in the amount of goods and services produced per head of the population over a period of time.

11. Which of the following is a cause of economic growth?
    a) A decrease in aggregate supply.
    b) A decrease in aggregate demand.
    c) An increase in aggregate supply (productive capacity).
    d) An increase in unemployment.
    Answer: c) An increase in aggregate supply (productive capacity).

GENERAL PRINCIPLES OF ECONOMICS

22. According to J.B. Say, Economics is the science of:
    a) Human behavior and resource allocation.
    b) Production, distribution, and consumption of wealth.
    c) Government intervention in markets.
    d) Prices and quantities of individual goods.
    Answer: b) Production, distribution, and consumption of wealth.

23. Which form of economics studies the decisions of people and businesses and their interaction in markets?
    a) Macro Economics
    b) Micro Economics
    c) Financial Economics
    d) Behavioral Economics
    Answer: b) Micro Economics

24. The goal of microeconomics is to explain:
    a) General price level and national income.
    b) Global economic systems.
    c) The prices and quantities of individual goods and services.
    d) Government spending and deficits.
    Answer: c) The prices and quantities of individual goods and services.

25. What does Macro Economics primarily study?
    a) Individual consumer choices.
    b) The national economy as well as the global economy.
    c) Specific market regulations.
    d) The behavior of a single firm.
    Answer: b) The national economy as well as the global economy.

26. Which of the following is a goal of macroeconomics?
    a) Explaining the supply and demand for a single product.
    b) Studying the effects of taxes on individual goods.
    c) Explaining general price level, national income, and employment.
    d) Analyzing the profit motive of private firms.
    Answer: c) Explaining general price level, national income, and employment.

MARKET STRUCTURES

27. A market structure characterized by an absence of competition and often resulting in high prices and inferior goods, containing a single firm, is called:
    a) Duopoly
    b) Oligopoly
    c) Monopoly
    d) Perfect Competition
    Answer: c) Monopoly

28. A situation in which two companies own all or nearly all of the market for a given product or service is known as:
    a) Monopoly
    b) Oligopoly
    c) Duopoly
    d) Monopolistic Competition
    Answer: c) Duopoly

29. Which market situation describes a particular market controlled by a small group of firms?
    a) Monopoly
    b) Duopoly
    c) Oligopoly
    d) Perfect Competition
    Answer: c) Oligopoly

30. In which market structure are there many buyers and sellers, and prices reflect supply and demand?
    a) Monopoly
    b) Oligopoly
    c) Duopoly
    d) Perfect Competition
    Answer: d) Perfect Competition

31. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of Perfect Competition?
    a) Many buyers and sellers.
    b) Consumers have many substitutes.
    c) New firms can easily enter the market.
    d) A single firm supplies a particular good or service.
    Answer: d) A single firm supplies a particular good or service.

32. A market structure where many firms sell products that are similar but not identical is called:
    a) Monopoly
    b) Oligopoly
    c) Perfect Competition
    d) Monopolistic Competition
    Answer: d) Monopolistic Competition

33. Which of the following is a characteristic of Monopolistic Competition?
    a) Only one seller.
    b) Homogeneous products.
    c) Product differentiation.
    d) High barriers to entry.
    Answer: c) Product differentiation.

ECONOMIC LAWS AND CONCEPTS

34. The law stating that people generally spend a smaller part of their budget on food as their income rises is known as:
    a) Okun's Law
    b) Gresham's Law
    c) Engel's Law
    d) Laffer Curve
    Answer: c) Engel's Law

35. The curve that represents the hypothetical relationship between rates of taxation and resulting levels of government revenue is the:
    a) Phillips Curve
    b) Kuznets Curve
    c) Laffer Curve
    d) Okun's Law
    Answer: c) Laffer Curve

36. Which law describes the relationship between an economy's unemployment rate and its gross national product (GNP)?
    a) Gresham's Law
    b) Phillips Curve
    c) Okun's Law
    d) Engel's Law
    Answer: c) Okun's Law

37. "Bad Money drives out good money" is a common statement of which economic law?
    a) Engel's Law
    b) Laffer Curve
    c) Gresham's Law
    d) Okun's Law
    Answer: c) Gresham's Law

38. The inverse relationship between the rate of inflation and the rate of unemployment is shown in the:
    a) Kuznets Curve
    b) Phillips Curve
    c) Laffer Curve
    d) Okun's Law
    Answer: b) Phillips Curve

39. The hypothesis that as an economy develops, market forces first increase and then decrease economic inequality is graphed by the:
    a) Phillips Curve
    b) Laffer Curve
    c) Kuznets Curve
    d) Okun's Law
    Answer: c) Kuznets Curve

40. What is a "Giffen Good"?
    a) A good whose demand decreases when its price increases.
    b) A good whose demand increases even after its price rises, violating the law of supply and demand.
    c) A good that always has many substitutes.
    d) A good primarily produced in the public sector.
    Answer: b) A good whose demand increases even after its price rises, violating the law of supply and demand.

INFLATION AND RELATED CONCEPTS

41. What is Inflation defined as?
    a) A decrease in the general level of prices.
    b) A period of no economic growth.
    c) The rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services is rising, and purchasing power is falling.
    d) An increase in the supply of money that is slower than the supply of goods.
    Answer: c) The rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services is rising, and purchasing power is falling.

42. Which type of inflation occurs when demand exceeds supply?
    a) Cost-Push Inflation
    b) Stagflation
    c) Demand-Pull Inflation
    d) Deflation
    Answer: c) Demand-Pull Inflation

43. An increase in the cost of raw materials leading to higher prices for goods is an example of:
    a) Demand-Pull Inflation
    b) Cost-Push Inflation
    c) Deflation
    d) Reflation
    Answer: b) Cost-Push Inflation

44. When the general prices of goods and services of an economy fall for a significant period of time, it is called:
    a) Inflation
    b) Stagflation
    c) Deflation
    d) Disinflation
    Answer: c) Deflation

45. An economic cycle characterized by a high rate of both inflation and unemployment is known as:
    a) Recession
    b) Depression
    c) Stagflation
    d) Disinflation
    Answer: c) Stagflation

46. What is the primary cause of high unemployment during stagflation?
    a) Increased consumer confidence.
    b) Economic contraction and slowing production of goods and services.
    c) High interest rates leading to increased investment.
    d) An increase in aggregate demand.
    Answer: b) Economic contraction and slowing production of goods and services.

47. An economic downturn where real GDP declines by more than 10% for a long period is defined as a:
    a) Recession
    b) Stagflation
    c) Depression
    d) Disinflation
    Answer: c) Depression

48. A period of general economic decline, typically accompanied by a drop in the stock market, an increase in unemployment, and a decline in the housing market, and generally less severe than a depression, is a:
    a) Depression
    b) Stagflation
    c) Recession
    d) Reflation
    Answer: c) Recession

49. Which of the following is NOT listed as a factor of recession?
    a) High interest rate
    b) Inflation
    c) Increased consumer confidence
    d) Reduced real wages
    Answer: c) Increased consumer confidence

50. What is "Disinflation"?
    a) A decrease in the general level of goods and services.
    b) A slow down in the rate of increase of the general price level of goods and services.
    c) A period of high inflation and high unemployment.
    d) Stimulating the economy by increasing the money supply.
    Answer: b) A slow down in the rate of increase of the general price level of goods and services.

51. The act of stimulating the economy by increasing the money supply or by reducing taxes, seeking to bring the economy back up to the long-term trend, is called:
    a) Deflation
    b) Disinflation
    c) Reflation
    d) Stagflation
    Answer: c) Reflation

SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT INDEX

78. The Human Development Index (HDI) was developed by which economist?
    a) Robert Giffen
    b) Arthur Okun
    c) Mahbub-ul-Haq
    d) Ernst Engel
    Answer: c) Mahbub-ul-Haq

79. When was the HDI developed?
    a) 1993
    b) 1857
    c) 1990
    d) 1957
    Answer: c) 1990

80. Which of the following is an indicator of the Human Development Index (HDI)?
    a) Wholesale Price Index (WPI)
    b) Index of Industrial Production (IIP)
    c) Life expectancy at birth
    d) Tax rates and government revenue
    Answer: c) Life expectancy at birth

81. What does "Knowledge" in HDI measurement encompass?
    a) Per capita income
    b) Adult literacy rate and combined gross enrolment ratio.
    c) Number of universities
    d) Technological advancements
    Answer: b) Adult literacy rate and combined gross enrolment ratio.

82. How is a "decent standard of living" measured in the HDI?
    a) Unemployment rate
    b) Gross Domestic Product (GDP) per capita at purchasing power parity (PPP) in US Dollars.
    c) Inflation rate
    d) Income inequality
    Answer: b) Gross Domestic Product (GDP) per capita at purchasing power parity (PPP) in US Dollars.

83. What does the IHDI (Inequality-adjusted Human Development Index) do?
    a) Measures only economic growth.
    b) Adjusts the HDI for inequality in the distribution of each dimension across the population.
    c) Focuses solely on health indicators.
    d) Replaces GDP with Giffen goods.
    Answer: b) Adjusts the HDI for inequality in the distribution of each dimension across the population.

GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP, NATIONAL INCOME

3.  What is GDP officially measured as?
    a) The total market value of all intermediate goods produced.
    b) The total output of goods and services in the economy within a nation's domestic border.
    c) The income earned by residents from overseas investments.
    d) The sum of consumption and investment only.
    Answer: b) The total output of goods and services in the economy within a nation's domestic border.

4.  In the GDP formula, what does 'C' stand for?
    a) Capital
    b) Cost
    c) Consumption
    d) Currency
    Answer: c) Consumption

5.  In the GDP formula, what does 'I' represent?
    a) Income
    b) Industry
    c) Investment
    d) Imports
    Answer: c) Investment

6.  In the GDP formula, what does 'G' stand for?
    a) Growth
    b) Goods
    c) Government Spending
    d) Gross
    Answer: c) Government Spending

7.  Which component is subtracted in the GDP formula to account for trade?
    a) (X + M)
    b) (X - M)
    c) (M - X)
    d) (G - I)
    Answer: b) (X - M)

8.  What does the word 'domestic' in GDP imply?
    a) Counting only goods produced by citizens.
    b) Counting goods produced within the nation's domestic border, regardless of who produced them.
    c) Counting only goods consumed domestically.
    d) Excluding goods produced by foreigners.
    Answer: b) Counting goods produced within the nation's domestic border, regardless of who produced them.

9.  GNP is the market value of all products and services produced in a year by whom?
    a) By all residents, including foreigners.
    b) By the citizens of a country.
    c) By public sector enterprises only.
    d) By foreign companies operating domestically.
    Answer: b) By the citizens of a country.

10. How is GNP calculated in relation to GDP, according to the text?
    a) GNP = GDP - (X - M)
    b) GNP = GDP + (X - M), where X is earned income in foreign countries by citizens and M is earned income by foreigners in a country.
    c) GNP = GDP + Depreciation
    d) GNP = GDP - Indirect Taxes
    Answer: b) GNP = GDP + (X - M), where X is earned income in foreign countries by citizens and M is earned income by foreigners in a country.

11. When would GDP be equal to GNP?
    a) When X > M
    b) When X < M
    c) When X = M
    d) When depreciation is zero
    Answer: c) When X = M

12. What type of economy would result if GDP is equal to GNP (X=M)?
    a) Open economy
    b) Mixed economy
    c) Closed economy
    d) Capitalist economy
    Answer: c) Closed economy

13. How is Net Domestic Product (NDP) calculated?
    a) GDP + depreciation
    b) GDP - depreciation
    c) GNP - depreciation
    d) NNP + subsidies
    Answer: b) GDP - depreciation

14. What does Net Domestic Product (NDP) account for?
    a) Income from abroad.
    b) Capital consumed over the year in the form of deterioration.
    c) Government spending.
    d) Indirect taxes.
    Answer: b) Capital consumed over the year in the form of deterioration.

15. How is Net National Product (NNP) derived?
